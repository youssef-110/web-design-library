# Pentagram

**In one line:** a design consultancy's homepage built as a white gallery wall, with one large photograph at a time.

## Colour
White canvas with near-black text (#1A1A1A). The editorial feature panel is charcoal (#222222) with a white heading and grey (#8C8C8C) body text. Colour comes only from the project photographs.

## Type
A single regular-weight grotesque, tightly set. The nav is 14px. The charcoal panel heading ("The Secret Life of Things") is about 36px in white, with small grey paragraph text beneath. Image captions are small white text laid over the photograph at its bottom left. The logo is the word "Pentagram" in a small serif at the top left.

## Layout and rhythm
A thin header: serif wordmark at left, links Work, About, News, Contact, a search icon and Archive at right. Under it a wide carousel photo spans the 1150px container with white margins either side; the caption (project name and one sentence) sits bottom left, and a row of dots sits bottom right. Next, a wide charcoal block with a heading and short copy occupies the same width, with an empty right half. White space separates the blocks generously.

## Shape, depth and texture
Everything is square-edged: photo, panel and buttons. There are no shadows or borders. The photograph in the capture is a close-up of a building facade with a lettering pattern against sky.

## Motion
Not observed. The row of dots shows the hero rotates through projects.

## Why it does not look generic
The interface is nearly empty: a studio that designs identities for others does not give itself a style. One big project image per screen makes the work the only visual statement, and the serif wordmark is the only decorative element.

## Steal this
- Use one full-width carousel image with a two-line caption in the corner as the entire hero.
- Switch to a charcoal panel with a white heading for editorial features, in the same container width as the images.
- Limit the header to a small wordmark and five text links so nothing competes with the work.

## Not for
Products that need to explain features or persuade with copy; the minimal frame offers no hooks without strong imagery.
