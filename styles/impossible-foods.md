# Impossible Foods

**In one line:** a plant-based meat brand shown as a loud, food-first poster in red on dark berry.

## Colour
The page background is a deep berry (#4F0423), not white. Red (#E10600) is used for the nav bar, headlines, buttons and the oversized footer wordmark. A pale pink (#FFC7C6) fills the "Meat Locator" panel, and a near-black wine (#260212) sets the FAQ panel and footer. Food photographs bring greens, yellows and oranges.

## Type
Two cuts of one heavy family. A wide extra-bold cut sets the main word ("STEAK", "MISSION", "FAQ'S"), and a tall condensed cut sets the secondary line ("(FROM PLANTS)", "LOCATOR", card titles). Small kicker labels in wide caps (IT'S GIVING) sit above, flanked by tiny red glyphs. Buttons and nav use condensed caps.

## Layout and rhythm
The hero is centred type: kicker, huge two-line headline with a small plate of food sitting on top of the letters, then two red buttons. Four photos float in the four corners at different heights. Further down: a tabbed product carousel (Beef, Sausage, Chicken) with product packs, a recipe pair ("The Potluck"), a mission block with a speech-bubble sticker, a dark FAQ panel with accordion rows, a pink locator panel, and a footer capped by a giant red "IMPOSSIBLE" wordmark that bleeds off the bottom.

## Shape, depth and texture
The top nav is a full-width red pill with smaller dark pills for Foodservice and Find Us. Photos are cropped to wobbly, scalloped blobs instead of rectangles. Panels have curved top edges. No shadows; separation comes from flat colour.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
Food is shown as cut-out stickers around the headline, not as a banner photo. The berry-and-red pairing reads as meat and sauce, and the footer wordmark is so big that the brand name works as a graphic.

## Steal this
- Place four irregular-shaped photos in the corners around a centred headline instead of using one hero image.
- Use two widths of one type family: wide for the keyword, condensed for the qualifier.
- Run an oversized wordmark across the footer and let it bleed off the page.

## Not for
Brands that need a quiet or clinical tone, and pages that must be read at length; the loud type and wobbly crops would work against them.
