# Calendly

**In one line:** the scheduling tool's homepage is a single glowing gradient stage with a cream frame around it and a product demo sliding up from the bottom.

## Colour
The page is a warm cream (#FCFBF8) with deep navy (#071A31) text and nav buttons. The hero is a rounded panel that blends from deep blue at top left through lavender to pink at the bottom right, with white text. Inside the cream product card, the demo calendar uses pale blue and violet.

## Type
The headline "All the work around meetings, handled." is set in a medium-weight (500) grotesque at about 72px in white, centred over two lines with tight tracking. The paragraph under it is about 19px, white, centred.

## Layout and rhythm
A navy-on-cream nav row, then the gradient panel inset about 24px from the edges and rounded. Inside it, a centred stack: headline, paragraph, two white buttons (Sign up with Google, Sign up with Microsoft) with brand icons, a small underlined email link, then a row of four icon tabs with arrows and an active tab that glows blue. Below the tabs, a cream card about 920px wide, with copy on the left (title, text, Learn more link) and a live calendar widget on the right.

## Shape, depth and texture
The tabs are rounded squares in frosted translucent white; the active one is solid blue with a pointer notch connecting it to the card below. The card has large radius and sits on the panel with a notch, so tab and card read as one tabbed object.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
The nav and panel framing make the page look like a card inside a card, and the tab strip is physically attached to the product demo by a notch, so the demo seems to belong to the tab you picked. The gradient panel replaces the usual flat colour hero.

## Steal this
- Set the hero inside an inset rounded gradient panel on a cream page, so the colour feels like a framed object.
- Connect a row of icon tabs to the product card below with a small pointer notch, so the switcher and demo read as one component.
- Offer sign-up with Google and Microsoft as the two white primary buttons, with email as a small text link beneath.

## Not for
Brands that want a flat, print-like look.
