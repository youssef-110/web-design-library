# Duolingo

**In one line:** a single-purpose landing screen that behaves like a game start menu: mascot, one sentence, two buttons.

## Colour
White canvas and a soft charcoal (#4B4B4B) headline. Colour comes from two places: leaf green (#58CC02) on the main button and in the owl mascot, and sky blue (#1CB0F6) as the text colour of the secondary button.

## Type
One rounded bold sans (Duolingo-Sans) throughout. The headline "The most fun way to learn languages, chess, and more!" is about 32px, bold, centred in two lines at the right of the illustration.

## Layout and rhythm
No hero banner and no scroll content above the fold. The logo sits top left, a grey site-language dropdown top right, and the vertical middle holds a two-column composition: illustration left, headline and stacked buttons right, both centred on the same horizontal line. The bottom edge has a fixed bar with a row of flags and subject icons (English, Chess, Math, Spanish and so on) between small chevrons.

## Shape, depth and texture
The Get Started button is green with a visibly darker lip along the bottom edge, like a physical key, and the I Already Have An Account button is white with a pale grey outline and the same thicker bottom edge. Both are wide rounded rectangles, not pills. The artwork is flat vector characters in a scattered, falling arrangement around the owl and a gold-coin tablet.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
Instead of a marketing hero, it drops the visitor straight at a decision: start or log in. The thick-bottomed buttons make every click feel like pressing a physical key, which carries the app's game language onto the web.

## Steal this
- Give primary and secondary buttons a thicker darker bottom border to simulate a pressable key.
- Reduce the landing screen to one illustration, one sentence and two buttons when the goal is a single action.
- Pin a horizontal strip of choices (languages here) to the bottom of the viewport so the offer of variety is visible without scrolling.

## Not for
Products that need to explain a complex offer before asking for sign-up.
