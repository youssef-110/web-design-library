# Mistral

**In one line:** a cream page with hairline cells where the AI company's pixel-art sunset blocks and a tiny cat turn the hero into a platform game.

## Colour
The canvas is warm off-white (#FBFBF8) with near-black text (#18181B). The brand is a stack of warm colours: gold (#FFAF01), orange (#FF8204 and #FF5229), red (#E51300) and deep crimson (#C4001D), used as solid blocks and diamonds, never as gradients. Black is used for the Start building button; UI is otherwise neutral.

## Type
A slightly condensed, tight grotesk at about 56px for hero text, regular weight, centred in two lines, in black. The headline includes an inline pixel-art flag icon in place of a word, and a small version of the Mistral M mark sits in the nav. Body and links use Inter at 14 to 20px.

## Layout and rhythm
Full-height cell grid defined by 1px lines: a left rail at 70 to 157px, a centre field and a right rail, with a nav that is itself divided into bordered cells (Research, Sovereign AI, Cloud, Customers, Company). The right edge shows a large menu list cropped by the viewport. A strip of customer logos (AXA, ASML, Cisco, Snowflake, HSBC, Ericsson) fills a row of equal bordered cells under the hero.

## Shape, depth and texture
Everything is square and flat. The hero scene uses 8-bit sprites: a cat on a gold-and-orange tower, a castle, a factory, orange diamonds, a hanging claw machine at top right. Fine arcs and dots are drawn as construction lines.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
The logo is a pixel stack of warm colours, and the page extends that grid into a world. The brand reads as a toy and an engineering diagram at once, unlike the blue-black AI norm.

## Steal this
- Derive a hero illustration from the logo's own geometry, such as pixel blocks, and tile it into the page margins.
- Divide nav, hero and logo strip with hairline cells so the whole page reads as a grid.
- Replace one word in the headline with an inline pixel icon.

## Not for
Brands that need austerity or luxury; the game-like sprites are too playful.
