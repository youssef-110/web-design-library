# Cartier, Le Chœur des Pierres

**In one line:** a high-jewellery campaign told as chapters of a story, where the stones and the silk they rest on carry the whole mood.

## Colour
Greyscale silk and white space surround the stones, which bring the only colour (sapphire blue, emerald green and others). Interface elements are black on white. The brand red appears only as a thin rule at the top of dialogs.

## Type
The title mixes cases and styles in one line: small connecting words ("le", "des") in an italic serif, the nouns in tall, high-contrast serif capitals, with "Chapter II" in small spaced capitals below. Labels and buttons use the house sans at 12px uppercase with +0.067em tracking; running text is a serif at 14 to 16px.

## Layout and rhythm
A centred script logo, a menu label top left and a sound toggle bottom right frame a full-screen scene. The title sits low and centred over the stones. Side controls (a pause and chapter markers) appear on the right edge. Text measures are capped at about 910px.

## Shape, depth and texture
Square buttons in solid black or 1px outlines, and photographic or rendered depth only: soft studio light, silk folds and the gloss of cabochon stones.

## Motion
Measured only at the entry: the site is a chaptered, scroll-driven film behind consent and location dialogs, and the probe saw the first scene. Interface transitions are short (0.15 to 0.3s colour and filter changes). A sound toggle suggests an audio layer.

## Rebuild notes
- Title lockup: articles and prepositions in an italic serif at about 60 percent of the cap height of the nouns; nouns in high-contrast serif capitals; chapter number in 12 to 14px spaced capitals (+0.1em) underneath.
- Labels and buttons: 12px uppercase, line height 1.5, +0.067em; solid black buttons with white text or 1px outlines, square.
- Scene: a full-bleed, slow-moving still or video of the product on a single fabric, greyscale except for the product itself.
- The probe could not get past the entry dialogs, so the story's scroll mechanics are not recorded here. Measure them in a real browser before relying on them.

## Why it does not look generic
The product is the only colour on the page, so the eye goes straight to the stones. The mixed-case title reads like an engraved invitation.

## Steal this
- Desaturate everything except the product so its colour becomes the composition.
- Set a title with small italic connecting words and capitalised nouns for an engraved, ceremonial voice.
- Structure a campaign as numbered chapters with a quiet sound toggle.

## Not for
Products without visual richness of their own. Utility pages and shops where people need to compare and act quickly.
