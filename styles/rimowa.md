# Rimowa

**In one line:** a luggage maker that puts its own product in the centre of a fairytale diorama and keeps the shop interface to thin white lines.

## Colour
The page is almost wholly photographic: silver aluminium, a blue painted sky, green moss hills and warm parquet floor. All chrome (nav, logo, headline, link) is white over the picture. The blue #0C67E7 and red #B50000 appear in the stylesheet as link and notice colours but nothing in the first view uses them; the ink colour #0D0900 is a very warm black for later pages.

## Type
Rimowa Grotesk in a light weight throughout. The headline is about 64px, regular-to-light, with generous letter spacing, centred on two lines. Navigation is small uppercase with wide tracking (around 0.1em), eight to ten categories spread evenly across the full width. The wordmark is thin, geometric, wide-set capitals.

## Layout and rhythm
Strict central axis. A utility row (country and language left, wordmark centre, boxed search field plus three icons right), then the category row, then the headline pinned in the lower middle and an underlined small-caps link below it. The suitcase sits left of centre so its open lid and landscape fill the upper two thirds, while the headline lands over the base and floor. A small white Live Service tab sits at the bottom right corner.

## Shape, depth and texture
Search is a 1px white rectangle with a transparent fill; buttons elsewhere are 4px rounded and outlined in blue. Depth comes entirely from the studio photo, with a shallow focus, warm side light and the ribbed grooves of the case, which act as the brand's texture.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
Luxury travel brands show a suitcase on white. Rimowa shows a suitcase carrying a miniature world, a story about what travel contains, and the signature grooved aluminium becomes the frame for the artwork.

## Steal this
- Let the product act as a stage: place a small handcrafted scene inside it so the hero image carries narrative.
- Use light weight plus wide tracking for uppercase navigation so a ten-item menu feels quiet over busy imagery.
- Put the headline over the lowest, plainest part of the photo, and underline a single small link instead of a filled button.

## Not for
Value or utility shops where shoppers need price and filters in the first view.
