# teenage engineering

**In one line:** a hardware maker's store that spends its whole first screen on one rendered object and then talks to you like a deadpan comic.

## Colour
The page is black with a pale grey floor only around the product, and almost every other pixel sits between #000000 and #131313. Type and line icons are white to light grey (#E5E5E5). The only colour comes from the product render itself (orange-red knobs and buttons) and a small green padlock icon at the right edge.

## Type
A single thin, wide, squarish face (Te-20) is used for everything: nav labels, the price, buttons and the jokes. The headline copy is set in capitals and centred in short stacked lines. Navigation items are small lowercase labels under larger ones, with a block of tiny Japanese text beside them.

## Layout and rhythm
The header is a row of line pictograms (a flower, a bag, a framed square, a magnifier with a dial) each with a word and three sub-links, and an outlined hex-nut logo at the far right. Below it one centred product render occupies the first screen. A centred caption, the price ($399) and a small outlined "AGREE!" button sit directly under it. The remaining 7,000 or so pixels are shadow and empty black, broken by a "HOLD ON..." button and a centred paragraph of jokes near the bottom.

## Shape, depth and texture
Buttons are 1px outlined rectangles with square corners. All depth is photographic: a soft spotlight on a grey floor and a hard, very long shadow cast from the object straight down the page. Icons are thin outline drawings.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
The page is mostly a shadow. Instead of filling the scroll with feature blocks, the product's own render supplies the length of the page and the copy jokes about it. The outline pictogram nav treats the menu like a set of device labels.

## Steal this
- Render the hero object once and extend its cast shadow down the page so the scroll itself becomes the visual.
- Replace text-only navigation with a row of outline pictograms, each with a word and three sub-links, so the menu reads like a control panel.
- Put the price and a single outlined button directly under the product with nothing else in between.

## Not for
Stores with many products or heavy comparison needs; the page has one object and almost no content. Also unsuitable where the user must scroll quickly to find information.
