# mymind

**In one line:** a private memory app that opens with a poster-sized serif promise and then shows what it holds: photos, quotes and prices on one pinboard.

## Colour
The page is near-white (#F9FAFC) with black type. All warmth sits in the hero halo, which fades from orange (#FF5924) through coral-pink to lilac and sky blue around a white disc, plus a solid orange Sign up pill. Tag chips in the subtitle each have their own pastel outline (orange, pink, pale blue, coral, yellow). Inside the board, the photos bring the colour.

## Type
An elegant, high-contrast serif at about 140px, set tight in two lines and centred, in near-black. Under it the body is a rounded sans at about 20px in grey. The screenshot's search field is an italic serif, "Search my mind...". Serif versus rounded sans separates feeling from function. The tokens list Avenir as heading, but the screenshot shows the serif.

## Layout and rhythm
A centred 1050px frame on a faint grey page, with a minimal nav: logo left, three dotted-colour links centre, Log in and an orange pill right. The headline fills the circle, then a sentence with pastel chips, then three store pills (iPhone, extension, Android), then a bordered board with tabs and a masonry grid of mixed cards, with a phone overlapping the bottom right.

## Shape, depth and texture
Soft, round shapes: the halo circle, pill chips, rounded 12px cards. The board is a masonry of real content: a chair, trainers, jewellery, a Mark Twain quote, a to-do list, a price tag overlay. Shadows are faint.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
It treats a notes tool as a gallery: the sample content has art-directed taste, and the headline is set like a magazine cover instead of a SaaS slogan.

## Steal this
- Place the headline over a white circle with a coloured gradient bleed so the type sits in a spotlight.
- Give each word in a chip row its own pastel outline colour, matching the nav dots.
- Fill the product mockup with beautiful, varied real-world content in masonry so it demonstrates the benefit.

## Not for
Technical audiences that expect specs; the poetic tone gives no detail.
