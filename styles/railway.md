# Railway

**In one line:** an infrastructure product that opens like a film poster: painted dusk clouds, a serif headline, and a calm dark dashboard.

## Colour
The page is a deep blue-violet black (#13111C) from top to bottom. Purple (#553F83) fills the primary Deploy button and is the only saturated UI colour. Sections are separated by subtly different tinted panels (blue-grey, teal-green, maroon), each barely lighter than the canvas. The hero sky adds dusty pink, mauve and navy.

## Type
The headline is IBM Plex Serif at about 54px in white, two centred lines, a deliberate break from the sans-serif norm in developer tools. Subtitle and UI are Inter around 20px, light grey. Section headings repeat the serif at about 36px, left aligned.

## Layout and rhythm
Slim top nav, then a wide rounded frame holding a full-bleed illustration. The headline, one line of copy and two buttons are centred on it, and an app window (project canvas with one backend node) rises from the frame bottom. Below, a logo strip, then long sections on a vertical timeline line, each with serif title, a large product screenshot and a three-up row of short feature notes.

## Shape, depth and texture
Corners are small. Depth comes from translucent dark surfaces and 1px borders on a painted anime-style sky, grainy and hand-illustrated. Screenshots are real dark UI (graphs, logs).

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
Cloud art and a serif face give an infrastructure tool a storybook mood, matching the "train" and journey metaphor instead of the usual terminal-green or gradient mesh.

## Steal this
- Use a serif display face over an otherwise sans UI to make a technical product feel authored.
- Put a real product window inside an illustrated scene so it rises from the bottom edge, anchoring the fold.
- Tint each long section with a slightly different dark hue, with one thin vertical line connecting them.

## Not for
Brands needing a bright, trustworthy or clinical tone.
