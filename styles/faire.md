# Faire

**In one line:** a wholesale marketplace that dresses like a boutique magazine, with each section announced by a block of colour.

## Colour
The base is warm off-white (#FBF8F6) with charcoal text (#333333) and a charcoal "Sign up" button. Between product rows the page drops in full-width bands: deep plum (#41252A), olive (#595604), pale lime (#F1F29F) and peach (#FFD0B6) at the bottom. Each band holds a short serif statement and photographs; the shop and product images supply the rest of the colour.

## Type
Headlines are a light, high-contrast serif ("Find your next bestseller", "Featured brands", "For any retailer, no matter what you sell"), set on one or two lines and often centred inside the colour bands. Interface text is a small neutral grotesque. The wordmark is spaced capitals (F A I R E) in a thin serif.

## Layout and rhythm
A promo strip, a header with a wide pill search field, a text row of category links (New products, Home decor, Women, Pets, Books), then a full-bleed hero photograph of a boutique with a serif headline at left. A sign-up modal covers the hero in the capture. Below: a "Featured brands" row with pill filters over six portrait photos, a plum band, a "Bestsellers" product rail, an olive band with a photo mosaic, three large category tiles, a "Shop by your values" filter block, a lime band with a photo, a four-card blog row and a peach closing band.

## Shape, depth and texture
Corners are 4px on buttons and cards; filters and search are pills. No shadows beyond the modal. Photography shows real shop owners and interiors, warm and slightly desaturated.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
Wholesale marketplaces usually show a dense grid first. This page alternates product rows with colour bands that each make one claim, with owner portraits instead of icons, so the content reads as stories about shops.

## Steal this
- Alternate dense product rails with single-colour bands that carry one serif sentence and one photo.
- Use pill filters above a photo row to switch categories in place.
- Pair a light serif headline with a small grotesque UI font so the interface stays quiet.

## Not for
Data-heavy tools or brands without human photography; the bands need strong portraits to work.
