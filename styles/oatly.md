# Oatly

**In one line:** an oat drink company that presents its homepage as a framed, zine-like desktop window with chunky cut-paper type over a grainy film photo.

## Colour
Cream graph-paper background (#FFFEF9) with faint square grid lines, white panels, and black everywhere for rules, labels and buttons. Colour arrives through photography (a green matcha drink, orange and red polaroids) and a small pale pink tag (#F8C8D8) bottom right. The tokenised red #E43E39 belongs to links, and nothing in the first view uses it. The tokens list a body face, but the screenshot shows the display and UI faces matter more.

## Type
The headline is set in a rough, hand-cut, ultra heavy all-caps face at about 200px, stacked into four left-aligned lines, white on the photo, with leading tighter than the cap height. The nav and every label are small uppercase monospace at about 12px. The contrast between a giant crude display and tiny typewriter text is the whole voice.

## Layout and rhythm
The page is drawn as an application window: a 1px black frame inset about 25px from the edge, an icon bar on top (home glyph, an empty address-like field, FAQ, B2B, globe, close), then a four-item uppercase nav spread evenly, then a full-width image panel, then a caption strip at the bottom with a report title on the left and the pink section tag on the right. The headline takes the left half and the photo subject takes the right.

## Shape, depth and texture
All corners are square, all lines are 1px black, buttons are black or white boxes with a rule. Texture comes from the grain and soft focus of the film photo and from the graph-paper ground. There are no shadows apart from a small popup.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
Food brands tend toward soft, rounded and pastel. Oatly goes anti-polish: rough lettering, desktop-window chrome and hand-written polaroid captions make a corporate site feel made by people.

## Steal this
- Draw the page as a framed window with a 1px rule border, an icon toolbar and a caption footer to give a product site editorial structure.
- Set one gigantic rough display line against tiny monospace uppercase labels for extreme scale contrast.
- Put a faint graph-paper grid behind white panels to add texture without images.

## Not for
Serious or regulated services (health, banking) where jokes in the chrome reduce trust.
