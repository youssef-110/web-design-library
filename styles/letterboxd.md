# Letterboxd

**In one line:** a social film diary that makes a dark, cinema-lobby page with a newspaper-style headline and a rail of posters.

## Colour
A blue-tinted charcoal (#14181C) fills the page, with pale blue-white text (#DDEEFF) and slate greys (#667788, #556677) for secondary copy. The only saturated UI colour is the green (#00AC1C) of the Get started button, which matches the green dot in the three-dot orange, green and blue logo. The posters and the hero still supply the rest, and the still fades into the page colour at its edges.

## Type
A heavy transitional serif at about 36px carries the three-sentence pitch, centred, with short parallel clauses on separate lines. Navigation and small labels are uppercase grotesque at 13px with tracking. Sub text is 20px grey. The logo wordmark is a rounded bold sans.

## Layout and rhythm
A narrow, centred 950px column. Logo and nav row at top, a tall still behind them that dissolves into the dark by the headline, then headline, button, a one-line product description, and finally a row of six posters at 150px width and 225px height with 10px gaps. Credit text for the still runs vertically along the right edge.

## Shape, depth and texture
Posters are small rectangles with 3px radius and a hairline border, the first poster has a gold outline. The hero still has a strong vignette and warm orange window light, which adds a cinematic, film-grain feel to otherwise flat UI.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
SaaS-style hero text is usually a single sans line. A confident serif list in the voice of a friend ("tell your friends what's good") and a still from a recent film make the app feel like a film club rather than software.

## Steal this
- Write the value proposition as three parallel short sentences, stacked and centred in a bold serif.
- Fade a hero still into the page colour with a vignette so the image has no visible edge.
- End the hero with a poster row of identical fixed-ratio tiles to show the content type immediately.

## Not for
Utility or enterprise tools where a warm serif and tiled artwork add no meaning.
