# Mailchimp

**In one line:** a marketing tool that keeps its old cheerful yellow, wrapped in a thick Intuit chrome and a photograph of a person at work.

## Colour
Three bands of neutral: a white Intuit app strip, a dark umber announcement bar (#231E15), a white nav. The hero is a photo darkened to a warm brown, so white text holds and the yellow (#FFE01B) of the button pops. The same yellow appears in the italic phrase of the headline, the Start Free Trial button in the nav, and an arrow circle further down.

## Type
The headline, about 64px, is a soft old-style serif, white, centred in two lines with the last three words ("that connect") in yellow italic. Body and navigation are a clean sans at 14 to 20px.

## Layout and rhythm
Stacked horizontal bands. Top: Intuit product strip with five tiny logos. Then a thin announcement bar, then a 68px nav with a monkey-badge logo, dropdowns, a search icon, a phone number, a black-outlined Log In and a yellow Start Free Trial. The hero then takes 650px with centred headline, paragraph, one large yellow pill and a review-badge row (Capterra, TrustRadius, G2). A dark band offers a product tour, and the page turns to light grey with a left-aligned section heading at a 320px indent.

## Shape, depth and texture
Pills for buttons, with a thin dark outline on the yellow. The photo has a desaturated tone, a monitor showing real product UI, an orange bike in the corner, and a man in a cap seen from the side, so the product appears as part of an actual workplace.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
The yellow italic inside a serif headline preserves the brand's friendly, editorial voice, while the parent company's strip and review logos show the corporate layer without letting it replace the personality.

## Steal this
- Colour the last phrase of a serif headline in the brand accent and set it in italic to mark the payoff.
- Darken a candid workplace photo to a warm brown so white type and a yellow button both pass contrast.
- Put third-party review logos directly beneath the first call to action, on the photo.

## Not for
Minimal or luxury brands; the stacked trust bands and badges create density.
