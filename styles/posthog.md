# PostHog

**In one line:** a developer analytics site presented as a retro desktop with a window, icons and a trash can.

## Colour
The wallpaper is a blurred green photo of grass and leaves (#A4BD7C). The window and menu bar are pale sage (#D3DAC6, #EEEFE9) with olive-black text (#23251D). Action buttons are amber (#EB9D2A) with a darker lower edge. A thick purple frame (#AE18D5) wraps the feature tab panel. Highlighted words in the headline carry a pale amber or blue marker stripe.

## Type
A friendly humanist sans in the headline "Your product's context layer" (about 26px at 1280 width), with two words highlighted in a marker-style box. Body copy is a regular small sans; monospace appears in technical labels.

## Layout and rhythm
A top menu bar holds a logo, Products, Pricing, Docs, Community, Company, More, and an amber "Get started, free" button at right. Two columns of desktop icons (Home, MCP, Self-driving, Pricing, Docs, Demo, Talk to a human at left; About us, Changelog, Company handbook, Store, Careers, Trash at right) sit over the wallpaper. In the centre a window about 800px wide holds the homepage: logo, headline and intro left, a "Set up for free" card with a checklist and two buttons at right, then a tabbed panel (Ask PostHog anything, Give agents product context, Ship with PostHog) framed in purple, with a prompt box left and copy right. The page continues inside the window with logos, numbered steps, and a closing CTA with a pixel-art hedgehog.

## Shape, depth and texture
Controls are small rounded rectangles with a hard offset bottom edge that makes buttons look pressed. Window chrome has tiny square buttons at the top. Photographic wallpaper and illustrated icons give it a tactile feel.

## Motion
Not observed.

## Why it does not look generic
The navigation metaphor is a real desktop: pages are icons, the footer links sit in a trash can, and the homepage is a window you could close. The product's own prompt box appears inside it.

## Steal this
- Present the site as an OS shell, with icons for sections and the page as a window, to give a tool a distinct world.
- Highlight key words in the headline with a marker-style box instead of bold.
- Give buttons a hard darker bottom edge so they read as physical keys.

## Not for
Conservative buyers or accessibility-critical flows; the icon-based navigation hides plain text links and demands more learning.
